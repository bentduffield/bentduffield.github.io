§1

Research

My work sits at the intersection of corpus linguistics and legal interpretation. I use corpus methods to examine ordinary-meaning doctrine and statutory textualism — asking not just what a disputed term meant to ordinary speakers at a given time, but how confidently a court, or a researcher, can claim to know that.

My dissertation work reframes intercoder reliability for corpus-based legal analysis in information-theoretic terms — moving past Krippendorff's α to incorporate item-level entropy, KL divergence between coder groups, and corpus-derived surprisal as a covariate. The aim is a measurement framework that tells courts and researchers not just whether coders agree, but how much that agreement is worth.
